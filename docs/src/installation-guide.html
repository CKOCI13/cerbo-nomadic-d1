Installation guide · control edition v1.3

Nomadic D1 DC-DC converter
on your Victron Cerbo GX

Make the cable, set up the Cerbo, and install the driver. When you're done, the D1's readings and controls appear on the Cerbo screen, in the VRM app and on a web page.

This is the control edition, for advanced users. It can command the D1. If you only want to see what the D1 is doing, use the monitor-only edition instead (github.com/CKOCI13/cerbo-nomadic-d1-monitor), which never sends the D1 anything.

What you'll see on the Cerbo screen when you're done (drawn from Victron's own screen design, so the real screen may differ slightly).

You'll be able to

  • See LV/HV voltage and current, temperature, status and alarms
  • Leave the D1 on its own Automatic logic, or take control with Off, Buck (48 V to 12 V) or Boost (12 V to 48 V)
  • Change the voltage targets and current limits

Good to know

  • It takes about 1 hour, including making the cable
  • Nothing changes until you choose. The Cerbo starts in Automatic, so the D1 keeps working exactly as it does today
  • You don't need to type any commands. The Cerbo Setup helper does it for you

1Get what you need

Gather everything before you start.

For the cable

  • A standard Ethernet patch cable (Cat5e or Cat6), long enough to reach from the Cerbo to the D1
  • Two crimp contacts to add CAN_H and CAN_L to the D1's 6-pin signal plug (TE Connectivity connector 1-967616-1, per the D1 manual). Ask Nomadic or a TE distributor for the matching plug and contacts, or for a ready-made pigtail
  • A ring terminal (M8) or a free spot on your ground bus, for the CAN ground wire
  • Wire cutters, a wire stripper and a crimp tool for the TE contacts
  • Heat-shrink tubing or electrical tape
  • A multimeter with a continuity (beep) setting and an Ohms setting
  • A VE.Can terminator: the small RJ45 plug that came in the Cerbo box
  • A 120 Ω resistor (¼ W) for the D1 end. Required: the D1 has no built-in terminator
  • Only if the D1 isn't wired yet: wire and a 3 A fuse for its KL30 and KL15 12 V wires (step 2f)

For the software

  • A Windows PC on the same network (Wi-Fi or Ethernet) as the Cerbo
  • The project folder downloaded from GitHub, which contains Cerbo Setup.bat and the dbus-nomadic-dcdc folder
  • The Cerbo running Venus OS v3.80 or newer. Step 3 shows you how to check

Safety

Before you work on the D1's wiring, switch off its 12 V supply (KL30/KL15) and follow the D1 manual. The CAN cable only carries data, but it ends next to the D1's 48 V and 12 V power terminals.

2Make the cable

The Cerbo uses RJ45 sockets, the same plug as network cables. The easiest way is to take a ready-made network cable, keep the plug on one end for the Cerbo, and cut off the other end. You only need 3 of its 8 wires: CAN_H and CAN_L go into the D1's signal plug, and the CAN ground goes to the common ground.

The whole connection

Blue: the network cable you make (CAN), with the required 120 Ω resistor across CAN_H and CAN_L at the D1. Green: CAN ground to the D1's common ground. Red: the D1's own 12 V wires (KL30 and KL15). If your D1 already works today, the red wires are already in place.

Which wire goes where

Pins 7 and 8 are the brown pair (twisted together), which suits CAN well. Pin 3 is white/green on most cables (T568B), or white/orange on T568A cables. The multimeter check in step 2b tells you which.

WireRJ45 pin (Cerbo)Goes to (D1)
white/brown7Pin 1 CAN_H on the 6-pin signal connector
brown8Pin 2 CAN_L on the 6-pin signal connector
white/green (white/orange on T568A)3Common ground: the D1's GND terminal, or the ground bus it's bolted to. The D1's signal connector has no ground pin
The other 5 wires (pins 1, 2, 4, 5, 6)Not used. Cut them short and insulate each one

Sources: Victron's VE.Can to CAN-bus BMS cables manual (Cerbo side: pin 7 CAN-H, pin 8 CAN-L, pin 3 GND) and the D1 Bidirectional DC-DC Converter Manual, Rev C (signal connector pinout).

Make it

2a
Cut off one plug. Keep the other plug for the Cerbo. Strip about 5 cm (2 in) of the grey outer jacket.
2b
Check the wires with a multimeter on the continuity (beep) setting. Touch one probe to a gold contact on the plug and the other to a wire:
  • Pin 7 should beep with white/brown
  • Pin 8 should beep with brown
  • Pin 3 should beep with white/green. If it beeps with white/orange instead, use white/orange as the CAN ground
2c
Insulate the 5 wires you don't need. Cut them short and cover each one separately with heat-shrink or tape, so none can touch anything.
2d
Add CAN_H and CAN_L to the D1's signal plug. With the D1 switched off, crimp contacts onto white/brown (into pin 1, CAN_H) and brown (into pin 2, CAN_L). Keep the brown pair twisted together as close to the plug as you can. If your D1 is already wired, unplug the existing signal plug and add the two contacts to it.
2e
Connect the CAN ground. Put a ring terminal on the white/green wire and connect it to the common ground: the D1's GND terminal, or the ground bus that terminal is bolted to.
Never connect any of the network-cable wires to the D1's HV+ or LV+ terminals. CAN_H and CAN_L only go to pins 1 and 2 of the signal plug.
2f
Check the D1's own 12 V wires (KL30 and KL15). The D1 needs both to work at all, and without them it's also silent on CAN. If your D1 already works today, they're already in place: just check them.
PinConnect to
4 KL30A permanent 12 V supply (9–16 V, up to 2 A) through a 3 A fuse close to the 12 V source
3 KL15A 12 V "enable" signal (8 V or more = on). Link it to KL30 to keep the D1 always enabled, or wire it through a switch, ignition or relay
5, 6Nothing. The manual says "do not connect"
2g
Fit the 120 Ω resistor at the D1. The D1 has no built-in terminator, so connect a 120 Ω resistor between CAN_H and CAN_L at the D1 end, for example between the white/brown and brown wires where they meet the signal plug. Insulate it with heat-shrink.
Check: with the RJ45 plug not yet in the Cerbo, measure between the white/brown and brown wires (or pins 7 and 8 of the RJ45 plug). It should read about 120 Ω.
2h
Final check: plug the RJ45 into the Cerbo port you'll use, with the VE.Can terminator in that port's other socket. With everything switched off, measure between white/brown and brown again:
You measureMeaning
About 60 ΩCorrect: both ends are terminated
About 120 ΩThe VE.Can terminator isn't in the Cerbo port's other socket, or the cable is in a different port
Very high or "OL"The resistor at the D1 isn't connected. Re-check step 2g

3Set up the Cerbo

Do this on the Cerbo's touchscreen, or remotely with Remote Console in VRM. Every item below is found from the Settings menu.

3a
Check the firmware version. Go to Settings › General › Firmware. It needs to be v3.80 or newer. If it's older, use Online updates on the same page to update first.
3b
Write down the Cerbo's IP address. Go to Settings › Connectivity › Ethernet (or Wi-Fi) and read the IP address.
IP address:
3c
Choose the CAN port and speed. Use the Cerbo's second CAN port, with nothing else plugged into it. The labels next to the ports tell you which Cerbo you have:
  • Cerbo GX MK2 (ports labeled VE.Can 1 and VE.Can 2): go to Settings › Connectivity › VE.Can port 2 (some firmware still calls it BMS-Can port) and set CAN-bus profile to Up, but no services (250 kbit/s). The D1 talks at either 250 or 500 kbit/s. Start with 250. Step 5c shows each port's speed, and tells you if the D1 is talking at the other speed. Then change the profile to Up, but no services (500 kbit/s).
  • Original Cerbo GX (second port labeled BMS-Can): go to Settings › Connectivity › BMS-Can port. This port only runs at 500 kbit/s and can't be set to "Up, but no services", so leave it on CAN-bus BMS (500 kbit/s). It works only if the D1 talks at 500 kbit/s. If step 5c reports bus errors on it, the D1 is at 250 kbit/s: use the VE.Can port (if nothing else is on it) with Up, but no services (250 kbit/s) instead.
3d
Let your PC connect to the Cerbo (this switches on SSH):
  1. Go to Settings › General › Access & Security › Access level and choose User & Installer. The password is ZZZ.
  2. Still on the Access & Security page, drag the list down with your finger and hold it for about 5 seconds (on Remote Console with a keyboard: hold the right-arrow key). The access level changes to Superuser.
  3. Tap Root password and choose a password (at least 8 characters).
    Root password:
  4. Switch Enable SSH on LAN to on.
3e
Optional, to change settings from VRM: go to Settings › VRM › VRM Portal access level and set it to Full.

4Connect everything

4a
Plug the cable's RJ45 plug into the CAN port you chose in 3c, with the VE.Can terminator in the port's other socket (as in step 2h).
4b
Plug the D1's signal plug back in and switch its 12 V supply (KL30/KL15) back on.
Nothing changes for the D1 yet. The Cerbo only listens until you finish Step 5, and even after that it starts in Automatic (it sends no commands).

5a
Open the helper. In the project folder, double-click Cerbo Setup.bat. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info and then Run anyway.
5b
Type the Cerbo's IP address from step 3b and press Enter. The helper remembers it for next time, and then shows its menu:

Want to see it first? Option 9 runs everything with a pretend D1 and sends nothing on CAN (see the Safe Test Plan, docs/safe-test-plan.pdf).
The password prompt. Every option that talks to the Cerbo asks for the root password from step 3d. Nothing appears on screen while you type it. That's normal: type it and press Enter.
5c
Type 2: find the converter. The helper copies the driver to the Cerbo and listens on every CAN port for 3 seconds. You should see something like this:
Venus OS v3.80 OK
Listening 3 s on each CAN port (nothing is transmitted)...
  can0    250 kbit/s  error-active  nothing from the converter
  can1    250 kbit/s  error-active  CONVERTER FOUND (391 frames)
The converter is on can1, which matches config.ini. Good.

If it says "In config.ini set: interface = can0", make that change in the next step. If the D1 is set to the other speed, you'll see bus errors instead:

  can1    250 kbit/s  error-passive nothing decoded, but 288 bus errors: talking at a different speed?
Something is talking on can1 at a different speed. On the Cerbo, set that port's CAN-bus
profile to "Up, but no services (500 kbit/s)" and run this again.

Do what it says (step 3c), then type 2 again. If nothing is heard at all, see Troubleshooting, and check the KL30/KL15 supply first.

5d
Type 1: edit the settings. Notepad opens config.ini. Check these lines, then press Ctrl+S to save and close Notepad:
LineWhat to put
interface = can1The port that step 5c found
startup = autoLeave as auto: after every Cerbo restart the D1 runs on its own logic
boost_voltage = 55.2The 48 V target used when you choose Boost. Set it to your 48 V battery's charge voltage

..._min / ..._max linesSafety limits. They're preset to the D1 manual's ranges; tighten them if your batteries, cables or fuses need it
custom_name = DC-DC D1The name shown on the Cerbo and in VRM
password =Optional: a password for the web page
Nobody can set a value outside the limits, whether from the Cerbo screen, VRM or the web page.
5e
Type 3: watch the live readings (optional, but a good check). The voltages should match what a meter shows on the D1's terminals. To stop, press Ctrl+C, then type N and press Enter.

No network to the Cerbo? Install from a USB stick instead

Venus OS can install add-ons from a USB stick or microSD card by itself, with no SSH or password. Do this instead of 5c–5f:

U1
In Cerbo Setup, type 1 and set interface (and anything else) in config.ini. The stick's settings replace the ones on the Cerbo. When Cerbo Setup asks for the IP address, you can just press Enter.
U2
Type U. It makes venus-data.tar.gz in the project folder. Copy it to the top folder of a USB stick or SD card (FAT32).
U3
Put the stick in the Cerbo and restart it (Settings › General › Reboot, or switch it off and on). The Cerbo unpacks the file and installs the driver while it starts, in Automatic.
U4
After about 2 minutes, take the stick out. While it stays in, every restart unpacks it again and puts its settings back. Then open the switch pane: the DC-DC D1 cards should be there.
Without SSH you can't run "find the converter" (5c). If the Status card says No communication, check the CAN port you chose in 3c, its speed (250 / 500 kbit/s) and the wiring, change interface if needed, and repeat U1–U4.

6Use it

On the Cerbo screen (and Remote Console)

Tap the switches icon at the top left of the screen. Two cards appear. Swipe left to see the second one.

DC-DC D1: live readings, the alarm and status, and the Direction dropdown.

DC-DC D1 settings: tap − / + to change a value. It's used whenever the Cerbo is in control (Buck or Boost).

Direction: who's in charge

ChoiceWhat happens
Automatic (default)The D1 decides by itself, as it always has. It bucks (charges the 12 V side from 48 V) when the 12 V side drops below 12.5 V, as long as the 48 V side is 41–58 V. It boosts (pushes 12 V energy up to 48 V) when the 12 V side is 13.5–16 V, until it falls below 12.3 V. Between 12.5 and 13.5 V it waits in Standby. The Cerbo sends nothing.
Buck 48V to 12VThe Cerbo makes the D1 charge the 12 V side, at Buck voltage (13.5 V) and up to Buck current limit. In Automatic the D1 uses its own factory target (about 13.4 V) instead.
Boost 12V to 48VThe Cerbo makes the D1 push energy to the 48 V side, at Boost voltage and up to Boost current limit.
OffThe Cerbo tells the D1 not to convert.

Changing between Buck and Boost always passes through Off for 1 second. Choosing Automatic again hands control back to the D1. The Alarm tile shows the most serious active alarm (red Fault label on a fault), and Status shows what the D1 reports it's doing, or No communication. The precharge controls are hidden because the D1 manual doesn't use precharge. To show them, or to rename or hide any item, go to Settings › Devices › DC-DC D1.

Limiting current to protect fuses and cables. Buck or Boost with a lower current limit can keep the D1 below what a fuse or cable can take, but only while the Cerbo keeps sending its command every 10 ms. When the commands stop (the Cerbo restarts or updates, the driver stops, or the CAN cable comes loose), it isn't yet known what the D1 does; it may go back to full current (up to 178 A into the 12 V side). So never fit a bigger fuse than the cable is rated for, size fuses and cables for the D1's maximum or ask Nomadic to lower its own limit, and only rely on the Cerbo's limit after the hand-back test below. The project README explains the settings.

Test the hand-back before relying on Off, Buck or Boost

The Safe Test Plan (docs/safe-test-plan.pdf) has the full careful version. In short: it isn't known yet whether the D1 goes back to its own logic when the Cerbo stops sending commands, or keeps following the last command. Do this test once, with the 48 V side between 41 and 58 V:

T1
Choose Buck. Status should change to Buck 48V to 12V.
T2
Choose Automatic. Within a few seconds Status should show what the D1 does on its own: Autonomous, Standby (12 V side 12.5–13.5 V), Buck (below 12.5 V) or Boost (13.5–16 V).
T3
Choose Buck again, then unplug the CAN cable at the Cerbo. With a meter on the D1's 12 V terminals, watch whether the D1 keeps regulating to 13.5 V, stops, or goes back to its own logic (about 13.4 V). Plug the cable back in.
If the D1 seems stuck on the last command (for example it stays in Standby in Automatic while the 12 V side is below 12.5 V), switch KL15 off and on to restart its controller, and tell Nomadic what you saw. Until you know how it behaves, leave the Cerbo in Automatic.

To change settings later, open Cerbo Setup, choose 1 to edit config.ini, save, then choose 4. The driver restarts with the new settings. Values you change on the screen, in VRM or on the web page are remembered automatically. To remove it, choose 8 and type YES. The D1 carries on in its own automatic mode.

Before you rely on Buck, Boost or Off, do the hand-back test in Step 6. While it's in control the Cerbo sends a command every 10 ms. If the Cerbo restarts or the cable comes loose, those commands stop, and it isn't yet known whether the D1 then goes back to its own logic.

Points to confirm with Nomadic

  • What the D1 does when CAN commands stop: does it go back to its own logic, and after how long? (Your hand-back test will show this too)
  • Whether it sends its status even when it isn't receiving commands (the driver assumes it does)
  • Reprogramming the factory buck target to 13.5 V, if you want 13.5 V in Automatic as well
  • Which direction counts as positive current in the readings
