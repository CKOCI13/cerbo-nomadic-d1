Safe test plan · control edition v1.4

Testing the D1 on the Cerbo,
one safe step at a time

Each stage only adds a little risk, and only after the stage before it has passed. Stages 1–4 can't change what the D1 does at all, because the Cerbo sends it nothing. Stop at the first thing that looks wrong.

Two kinds of Automatic. The shipped config.ini has automatic = limited: in Automatic the Cerbo chooses buck, boost or standby itself, by the D1 manual's rules, and sends your current limits with it. That is what owners use every day. For stages 3–5, set automatic = hands_off instead: then Automatic sends nothing and the D1 runs on its own logic, exactly as without a Cerbo. Stage 6 switches to limited and tests it.

What protects you

  • The D1 itself: it stops on 12 V side over 16–18 V or under 8 V, 48 V side over 58–60 V or under 24 V, over 213 A, a short, or a board temperature over 120 °C (D1 manual)
  • Your BMS and fuses on HV+, LV+ and KL30
  • The driver's limits in config.ini, and the test timer
  • KL15: your emergency stop. KL15 off shuts the D1's controller down immediately, whatever the Cerbo is doing. If KL15 is joined to KL30 on one wire (as on the LTV1), pulling that wire's fuse does the same

Before you start

  • Fuses on HV+ and LV+, 3 A on KL30 and 1 A on KL15 (D1 manual Rev D; one 3 A fuse if they share a wire)
  • KL15 through a switch you can reach (or a fuse you can pull)
  • A multimeter, plus a DC clamp meter if you have one
  • Know your batteries' maximum charge voltages (12 V chassis and 48 V)
  • The Cerbo's battery monitor in view (48 V current)
  • The values on your D1's label: Nomadic can configure each unit differently

1Simulation: no converter at all

In Cerbo Setup, choose 9. The real driver runs on your Cerbo against a pretend D1, and no CAN socket is ever opened. A real installation keeps running beside it.

CheckOK
"DC-DC D1 (SIMULATED)" appears in the Cerbo's switch pane, and in VRM's Switches
Its web page opens at http://<Cerbo IP>:8089
In Automatic (with limits) the pretend 12 V side slowly drops. Below 12.5 V, Status changes to Buck and it charges back to about 13.4 V, at no more than your Buck current limit. When the pretend engine starts (every 3 minutes) it switches to Boost; the web page says why each time
Buck: Status changes to Buck and the 12 V side climbs to your Buck voltage (13.5 V). Off: the current goes to zero
Boost: the 48 V side rises a little, the 12 V side drops, and boost stops at 12.3 V
Change the set-points and limits: values outside config.ini are refused

Stop with Ctrl+C. The simulated device disappears. The pretend D1 follows the manual's logic, but it can't tell you how the real one reacts to commands. That's what stage 5 is for.

2Wiring check, everything off

CheckOK
Everything off: the KL30/KL15 fuse pulled, and the fuses on LV+ and HV+ open
Continuity: RJ45 pin 7 to white/brown, pin 8 to brown (guide step 2b)
No continuity (no beep) between white/brown or brown and LV+, HV+ or GND
Lever connector A holds the D1's CAN_H wire, white/brown and one resistor leg; B holds CAN_L, brown and the other leg. Every wire fully in, levers closed, resistor legs insulated. White/green and the other unused wires insulated (no ground wire)
RJ45 not in the Cerbo: white/brown to brown reads about 120 Ω (your resistor at the D1)
RJ45 in the Cerbo, with the VE.Can terminator in the other socket: about 60 Ω

3Listen only

Power the D1 as normal (KL30 and KL15 on). In Cerbo Setup choose 2 (find), then 3 (watch). The Cerbo sends nothing.

CheckWhat you sawOK

4Installed in Automatic (hands off)

Choose 1, set automatic = hands_off, save, then choose 4 (install). The driver starts in Automatic and still sends nothing (the web page says "Automatic (hands off)"). Leave it running for a day.

CheckOK
Cards on the Cerbo, in VRM and on the web page, with sensible readings
The D1 behaves exactly as before: buck when the 12 V side is low, standby otherwise

5First commands, with guard rails

Prepare: in Cerbo Setup choose 1, set these test values (keep automatic = hands_off), save, then choose 4:

Conditions: 48 V side between 41 and 58 V; 12 V batteries healthy; someone at the KL15 switch; a meter on the D1's 12 V terminals. Do each step, watch for 1–2 minutes, and fill in the table.

Stop at once (KL15 off) if: any current goes above the limit you set; any voltage goes above your batteries' limits; a red Fault appears; the board temperature climbs fast (above about 80 °C); or anything smells, gets hot or sounds wrong. Then choose Off on the Cerbo before you switch KL15 back on. If the D1 then seems stuck on the last command, switch KL15 off and on to restart its controller.

6Automatic with limits

Prepare: choose 1, set automatic = limited and keep the small test limits from stage 5, save, then choose 4. Choose Automatic. The Cerbo now chooses buck, boost or standby by the D1 manual's rules (buck below 12.5 V, boost from 13.5 V until 12.3 V), waits 2 seconds before each change, and sends your limits every 10 ms. Keep a clamp meter on the 12 V cable and a hand on the KL15 switch.

#DoExpectWhat you sawOK
6aEngine off, 12 V side 12.5–13.5 VStatus: Standby. Web page: "Automatic with limits: Standby (Off)"
6bEngine off, loads on until the 12 V side is below 12.5 VA few seconds later: Buck. 12 V current ≤ 20 A; the 12 V side rises to about 13.4 V and stays there
6cStart the engineOnce the alternator lifts the 12 V side above 13.5 V: Standby, then Boost after a few more seconds. 48 V current ≤ 10 A; 12 V cable current ≤ about 5 × that
6dStop the engine while boostingBoost continues until the 12 V side falls below 12.3 V, then Off (Buck if it's below 12.5 V). No Boost again for 1 minute
6eAll through 6a–6dNever straight from Buck to Boost or back without Off between; the clamp meter never above your limit

Stop at once, as in stage 5, if anything goes wrong.

7Normal use

CheckOK
Set test_timer = 0, and set the current limits to what your wiring, fuses, batteries and the D1's label allow (option 1, then 4). Stock LTV1 (2 AWG, 150 A fuse): buck_current_limit_max = 120, boost_current_limit_max = 23
Put boost_voltage back to your 48 V battery's charge voltage
Keep automatic = limited and startup = auto, so the Cerbo is in Automatic with limits after every restart
Remember what you saw in 5g: while the Cerbo restarts or updates, the D1 may run on its own logic at its factory limits
